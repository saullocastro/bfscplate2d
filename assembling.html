5. Assembly, loads and boundary conditions#

bfscplate2d computes the contributions of one element. The mesh, the global matrices and vectors, the loads and the boundary conditions belong to the model, written by the user with NumPy and SciPy. This chapter states the conventions that connect the element of The BFSC plate element to a model.

5.1. Global degrees of freedom#

A model with num_nodes nodes has N = DOF*num_nodes degrees of freedom. The attributes c1 to c4 of each element give the position of the first degree of freedom of its nodes in the global vectors; the usual choice is c = DOF*i for the node of index i, with which the slice u[6::DOF] of a global vector gives \(w\) at all the nodes, u[0::DOF] gives \(u\), u[7::DOF] gives \(w_{,x}\), and so on, in the order of the table of The BFSC plate element.

A rectangular plate of sides \(a\) and \(b\) is divided into rectangles of sides lex and ley; with nx by ny nodes on a regular grid, lex = a/(nx - 1) and ley = b/(ny - 1). Each element takes its four nodes counterclockwise from the corner of smallest \(x\) and \(y\), the order of The BFSC plate element; a node shared by several elements has the same c in all of them, which makes the assembled fields continuous.

5.2. Sparse arrays#

Each element matrix is written as triplets, the row index, the column index and the value of each entry, in three 1-D arrays, the coordinate format of scipy.sparse.coo_matrix. The functions write the entries of one element at the positions init_k to init_k + SPARSE_SIZE - 1, with init_k the attribute of the element for that matrix and SPARSE_SIZE the number of entries per element. The sizes follow from the pairs of degrees of freedom that each matrix couples, with 24 in-plane degrees of freedom (\(u\) to \(v_{,y}\) of the four nodes) and 16 out-of-plane ones (\(w\) to \(w_{,xy}\)) per element:

For num_elements elements the arrays have SPARSE_SIZE*num_elements entries, of type INT for the indices and DOUBLE for the values, and the element of index k gets init_k = k*SPARSE_SIZE. The global matrix is the sum of the element matrices, \(\boldsymbol{K} = \sum_e \boldsymbol{L}_e^\top \boldsymbol{K}_e \boldsymbol{L}_e\), with \(\boldsymbol{L}_e\) the Boolean matrix that extracts \(\boldsymbol{u}_e\) from the global vector; coo_matrix performs this sum by adding the values at repeated positions:

KC0 = coo_matrix((KC0v, (KC0r, KC0c)), shape=(N, N)).tocsc()

The functions write all the pairs, not only a triangle, such that the symmetric matrices come out symmetric after the sum and the unsymmetric load stiffness matrix of Follower pressure is complete. The indices are overwritten, but the values are added to the existing ones: value arrays reused for a new configuration, e.g. those of KCNL and KG at each iteration of a nonlinear analysis, are zeroed first; the indices do not change for a fixed mesh. INT, DOUBLE, DOF and the sizes are given by the bfscplate2d namespace.

The vectors have no position attributes: update_fint() and update_fext_follower() add the vector of one element to a global vector of size N at the positions of its nodes, and every function that depends on the displacements reads them from a global vector u of size N.

5.3. Loads#

The external force vector is the virtual work of the loads, \(\delta W = \delta\boldsymbol{u}^\top \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\). A concentrated force or moment is a value at one degree of freedom of a node, e.g. a transverse force at the position c + 6; the conjugate of \(w_{,x}\) is a moment, that of \(w_{,xy}\) a generalised force of dimension force times length squared. Distributed loads give the consistent load vectors, integrated with the rows of The BFSC plate element and the quadrature of Element matrices and internal forces:

  • a dead pressure \(P\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \int_A P (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top dA\), with BFSCPlate2D.update_Sw() at the integration points; this is also update_fext_follower() at \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\);

  • an in-plane force per unit length \(\bar{N}_{xx}\) on an edge \(x\) = constant, \(\int \bar{N}_{xx} (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top dy\), with BFSCPlate2D.update_Su() at \(\xi = \pm 1\);

  • a follower pressure, update_fext_follower(), see Follower pressure.

The consistent load of a uniform edge load on an edge \(x\) = constant has, besides the forces at \(u\), entries at \(u_{,y}\) of the two nodes at the ends of the edge (those of the interior nodes cancel between neighbouring elements). Nodal forces at \(u\) alone, e.g. with half the force at the corner nodes, are not equivalent, and the membrane forces of the resulting static solution are not exactly uniform, see Analyses.

5.4. Boundary conditions#

A constrained degree of freedom is set to zero by partitioning the global system into the unknown degrees of freedom bu and the constrained ones bk = ~bu, two Boolean arrays of size N:

(5.1)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{uu} \boldsymbol{u}_u = \boldsymbol{F}_u\]

with the matrices restricted to bu by bu and the vectors to bu, e.g. KC0[bu, :][:, bu]. The solution is expanded to the size of the model, with zeros at the constrained degrees of freedom, u[bu] = uu, before it is given to the functions that depend on the displacements.

Since a degree of freedom is a nodal value, the condition it imposes along an edge follows from Eq. (2.8):

each value with its derivative along the edge. A simply supported edge, \(w = 0\) with a free normal slope, is therefore \(w\) and \(w_{,y}\) at the nodes of an edge \(x\) = constant (\(w\) and \(w_{,x}\) on an edge \(y\) = constant), with \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,xy}\) free; at a corner between two such edges \(w\), \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\) are constrained and \(w_{,xy}\) stays free, since the twist at the corner of a simply supported plate is not zero, as for the mode \(w = \sin(m \pi x/a) \sin(n \pi y/b)\). A clamped edge adds the normal slope with its derivative along the edge. Constraining the value alone, e.g. \(w\) at the nodes of a simply supported edge, makes it vanish at the nodes only: between them \(w\) is the cubic of Eq. (2.8) with the free nodal \(w_{,y}\), the edge is more flexible than a supported one, and the condition is reached only as the mesh is refined. On a coarse mesh of a few elements per side the difference is a fraction of a per cent in the deflections and in the buckling loads, and grows with the number of half-waves of a mode. The rigid-body motions must be constrained, in-plane and out-of-plane, for KC0[bu, :][:, bu] to be nonsingular. Prescribed nonzero displacements are a partition with u[bk] given and the right-hand side \(\boldsymbol{F}_u - \boldsymbol{K}_{uk}\boldsymbol{u}_k\).
