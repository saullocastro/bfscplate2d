2. The BFSC plate element#

BFSCPlate2D approximates the plate theory of Plate theory over a rectangle with four nodes. Its name comes from the element of Bogner, Fox and Schmit (BFS) [Bogner1965], for the deflection \(w\), whose interpolation the BFSC element extends to the in-plane displacements \(u\) and \(v\) [CastroJansen2021]. This chapter derives its interpolation functions, its degrees of freedom and the operators that give the strains; Element matrices and internal forces uses them to build the matrices.

2.1. Why the interpolation of \(w\) must be C1#

The curvatures of Eq. (1.2) contain the second derivatives of \(w\). The strain energy of an approximation of \(w\) built element by element is finite, and the approximation is conforming, only when \(w\) and its first derivatives \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\) are continuous across the edges between elements, i.e. when \(w\) is of class C1; within an element the second derivatives need only be integrable. Interpolations with the nodal values of \(w\) alone, as in the membrane elements, are C0: the slope normal to an edge jumps from one element to the next.

On a mesh of rectangles with sides parallel to the axes, C1 continuity is obtained with products of one-dimensional cubic Hermite polynomials, which interpolate a function from its values and first derivatives at the two ends of an interval [Bogner1965]. The products of the four Hermite polynomials along \(x\) with the four along \(y\) are 16 functions, which span all the bicubic polynomials, and need four degrees of freedom of \(w\) per node: \(w\), \(w_{,x}\), \(w_{,y}\) and the cross derivative \(w_{,xy}\), the nodal value multiplying the product of the two slope functions. This is the BFS element. The construction uses the constant mapping between the natural and the physical coordinates of a rectangle with sides parallel to \(x\) and \(y\), below, to which the element is restricted.

2.2. Geometry and natural coordinates#

The element has the sides lex along \(x\) and ley along \(y\). Its natural coordinates \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) (xi and eta) go from \(-1\) to \(1\):

(2.1)#\[x = x_1 + \frac{lex}{2} (\xi + 1), \qquad y = y_1 + \frac{ley}{2} (\eta + 1), \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial x} = \frac{2}{lex} \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial y} = \frac{2}{ley} \frac{\partial}{\partial \eta}, \qquad dA = \frac{lex \, ley}{4} \, d\xi \, d\eta\]

with \((x_1, y_1)\) the position of node 1. The nodes are numbered counterclockwise from the corner with the smallest \(x\) and \(y\), as the CQUAD4 element of Nastran:

2.3. Hermite polynomials and interpolation functions#

For a node at \(\xi_i = \pm 1\), the two cubic Hermite polynomials of that end of the interval \([-1, 1]\) are

(2.2)#\[f_i(\xi) = \frac{1}{4} (\xi + \xi_i)^2 (2 - \xi \xi_i), \qquad g_i(\xi) = \frac{1}{4} \xi_i (\xi + \xi_i)^2 (\xi \xi_i - 1)\]

with \(f_i(\xi_i) = 1\), \(f_i'(\xi_i) = 0\), \(g_i(\xi_i) = 0\), \(g_i'(\xi_i) = 1\), and \(f_i\), \(f_i'\), \(g_i\), \(g_i'\) all null at the other end, \(\xi = -\xi_i\): \(f_i\) carries the value and \(g_i\) the derivative with respect to \(\xi\) at the node. The interpolation functions of node \(i\) are their products, with the factors \(lex/2\) and \(ley/2\) that turn a derivative with respect to \(\xi\) or \(\eta\) into one with respect to \(x\) or \(y\):

(2.3)#\[H_i = f_i(\xi) f_i(\eta), \qquad H_{x,i} = \frac{lex}{2} g_i(\xi) f_i(\eta), \qquad H_{y,i} = \frac{ley}{2} f_i(\xi) g_i(\eta), \qquad H_{xy,i} = \frac{lex \, ley}{4} g_i(\xi) g_i(\eta)\]

Each function is 1 for the nodal degree of freedom it multiplies, e.g. \(\partial H_{x,i}/\partial x = 1\) and \(\partial^2 H_{xy,i}/\partial x \partial y = 1\) at node \(i\), and 0 for every other nodal degree of freedom of the element. Expanded, Eq. (2.3) is the form given in the Notes of bfscplate2d.bfscplate2d [OchoaReddy1992], e.g. \(H_i = (\xi + \xi_i)^2 (\xi \xi_i - 2) (\eta + \eta_i)^2 (\eta \eta_i - 2)/16\). The functions are bicubic, of degree 3 in \(\xi\) and in \(\eta\), which sets the number of integration points of each matrix, see Element matrices and internal forces.

2.4. Degrees of freedom and interpolation#

In the BFSC element the in-plane displacements are interpolated with the same functions as \(w\), without the cross derivatives [CastroJansen2021]:

(2.4)#\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}u = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i u_i + H_{x,i} u_{,x i} + H_{y,i} u_{,y i} \right), \qquad v = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i v_i + H_{x,i} v_{,x i} + H_{y,i} v_{,y i} \right),\\w = \sum_{i=1}^{4} \left( H_i w_i + H_{x,i} w_{,x i} + H_{y,i} w_{,y i} + H_{xy,i} w_{,xy i} \right)\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

The 40 degrees of freedom of the element, the vector \(\boldsymbol{u}_e\) (ue in the code, the attribute u of the element), are those of the nodes 1, 2, 3 and 4 in sequence, 10 per node in the order of the table. In matrix form

(2.5)#\[u = \boldsymbol{S}^u \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad v = \boldsymbol{S}^v \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad w = \boldsymbol{S}^w \boldsymbol{u}_e\]

with the rows of 40 entries Su, Sv and Sw, computed at a point \((\xi, \eta)\) by BFSCPlate2D.update_Su(), BFSCPlate2D.update_Sv() and BFSCPlate2D.update_Sw().

Every function of Eq. (2.3) has a null cross derivative at the four nodes, except \(H_{xy,i}\) at node \(i\). Without \(H_{xy,i}\), the interpolation of \(u\) and \(v\) therefore gives \(u_{,xy} = v_{,xy} = 0\) at the nodes: the 12 functions of \(u\) (and of \(v\)) contain the linear polynomials, hence the states of constant membrane strain, and e.g. \(x^2\) and \(y^3\), but not \(xy\), whose cross derivative is 1 everywhere.

The cross derivatives are left out by the design of the element [CastroJansen2021]. The BFS element, with 6 degrees of freedom per node, interpolates \(u\) and \(v\) linearly, which converges poorly for the second-order displacement fields of Koiter’s method of initial post-buckling and for the quantities computed from them. The BFSC element was made to reach a third-order interpolation also for \(u\) and \(v\) with the 4 nodes of the BFS element, by adding to each node only the first derivatives \(u_{,x}\), \(u_{,y}\), \(v_{,x}\) and \(v_{,y}\), which gives 10 degrees of freedom per node; a node carrying \(u_{,xy}\) and \(v_{,xy}\) too would have 12.

The degrees of freedom mix lengths, dimensionless slopes and the cross derivative of dimension one over length. Norms of vectors of degrees of freedom, e.g. in a convergence criterion, depend on the units unless they are scaled, see Analyses.

2.5. Derivatives and strain operators#

With the constant mapping of Eq. (2.1), the derivatives of the rows are

(2.6)#\[\boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} = \frac{2}{lex} \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{S}^u}{\partial \xi}, \quad \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,y} = \frac{2}{ley} \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{S}^u}{\partial \eta}, \quad \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,xx} = \frac{4}{lex^2} \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{S}^w}{\partial \xi^2}, \quad \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,xy} = \frac{4}{lex \, ley} \frac{\partial^2 \boldsymbol{S}^w}{\partial \xi \partial \eta}, \quad \ldots\]

the rows Su_x, Su_y, Sv_x, Sv_y, Sw_x and Sw_y of BFSCPlate2D.update_Su_x() to BFSCPlate2D.update_Sw_y(). Substituted in Eq. (1.2), they give the strains in terms of the element degrees of freedom:

(2.7)#\[\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m + \frac{1}{2} \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \right) \boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \left( \boldsymbol{B}_m + \boldsymbol{B}_{NL} \right) \delta\boldsymbol{u}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{\kappa} = \boldsymbol{B}_b \boldsymbol{u}_e\]

Since the nonlinear strains are quadratic in \(\boldsymbol{u}_e\), \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\boldsymbol{u}_e\) is twice the nonlinear strains, whereas their variation is \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\delta\boldsymbol{u}_e\), hence the factors \(1/2\) and \(1\) of Eq. (2.7). \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\), \(\boldsymbol{G}\) and \(\boldsymbol{B}_{NL}\) have nonzero entries only in the 16 columns of \(w\), \(w_{,x}\), \(w_{,y}\), \(w_{,xy}\), and \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) only in the 24 columns of \(u\) to \(v_{,y}\). In the functions of the module the nonzero entries are local variables named after the operator, the row and the column, both counted from 1: Bm1_01 is the entry of \(\boldsymbol{B}_m\) in the row 1 and the column 1 (\(u\) of node 1), Bb3_40 the entry of \(\boldsymbol{B}_b\) in the row 3 and the column 40 (\(w_{,xy}\) of node 4).

The update_* functions of the module evaluate the operators themselves and do not use the rows stored in the element. The methods of the element serve the user, e.g. to integrate a distributed load with Sw or Su, see Assembly, loads and boundary conditions, or to recover from a solution the linear membrane strains Bm @ ue and the curvatures Bb @ ue at a point, with ue the 40 degrees of freedom of the element, and from them the membrane forces and moments of Eq. (1.3).

2.6. Continuity between elements#

On the edge \(\xi = \xi_i\) of an element, \(f_j(\xi_i)\) is 1 for the two nodes \(j\) of that edge and 0 for the other two, and \(g_j(\xi_i) = 0\) for all nodes. Along the edge, Eq. (2.4) reduces to

(2.8)#\[w = \sum_{j \in \text{edge}} \left( f_j(\eta) w_j + \frac{ley}{2} g_j(\eta) w_{,y j} \right), \qquad w_{,x} = \sum_{j \in \text{edge}} \left( f_j(\eta) w_{,x j} + \frac{ley}{2} g_j(\eta) w_{,xy j} \right)\]

and likewise for an edge \(\eta\) = constant with \(x\) and \(y\) exchanged. \(w\) along an edge, and its slope normal to the edge, depend only on the degrees of freedom of the two nodes of the edge, shared by the two neighbouring elements: \(w\), \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\) are continuous, and the element is conforming for the classical plate theory. By the same argument \(u\), \(v\) and their first derivatives are continuous; along an edge \(x\) = constant, \(u_{,x}\) is interpolated from the nodal \(u_{,x}\) with \(f_j(\eta)\) only, since \(u_{,xy}\) is not a degree of freedom. The second derivatives, i.e. the curvatures and the moments, are in general discontinuous between elements.

Eq. (2.8) also shows which constraints make a displacement vanish along a whole edge, see Assembly, loads and boundary conditions: \(w = 0\) along an edge \(x\) = constant needs \(w = 0\) and \(w_{,y} = 0\) at its nodes, and a zero normal slope \(w_{,x} = 0\) needs \(w_{,x} = 0\) and \(w_{,xy} = 0\).

2.7. Attributes of the element#

Before the functions of the module are called, the attributes below are set for each element, see BFSCPlate2D for their definitions:

  • c1, c2, c3, c4: positions of the first degree of freedom of each node in the global vectors;

  • lex, ley: lengths of the sides;

  • ABD: the (6, 6) constitutive matrix of Eq. (1.3), constant over the element;

  • h and rho: thickness and density, read only by update_M();

  • init_k_KC0, init_k_KCNL, init_k_KG, init_k_M, init_k_KCfollower: positions of the element in the sparse arrays of each matrix, see Assembly, loads and boundary conditions.

n1, n2, n3 and n4 are identification numbers for the user, not read by the functions. A laminate that varies over the plate is represented by a different ABD in each element.
