6. Analyses#

The matrices and vectors of Element matrices and internal forces and Follower pressure, assembled and restricted to the unknown degrees of freedom as in Assembly, loads and boundary conditions, give the equations of four analyses:

\((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G)\boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\)

\((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} - \omega_n^2 \boldsymbol{M})\boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\)

The library solves none of them: the systems are solved with SciPy or with structsolve, see Solving with structsolve.

6.1. Linear static analysis#

For small displacements the internal forces of Eq. (3.2) reduce to \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\boldsymbol{u}\), and the equilibrium is

(6.1)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) symmetric, positive definite once the rigid-body motions are constrained, e.g. solved with scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve. The membrane and bending problems are coupled only through \(\boldsymbol{B}\): for \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\) in-plane loads give no deflection and transverse loads no membrane forces.

6.2. Linear buckling analysis#

Under in-plane loads \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) a flat plate with \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\) has the linear pre-buckling state \(\lambda \boldsymbol{u}_0\), \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\boldsymbol{u}_0 = \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), without deflection. Its slopes are null, hence \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} = \boldsymbol{0}\), and the tangent stiffness matrix of Eq. (3.4) along this path is \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u}_0)\), since \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is linear in the displacements. A bifurcation occurs where it becomes singular:

(6.2)#\[(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_G) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

with the load multipliers \(\lambda\), the critical loads being \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), and the buckling modes \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\). A negative \(\lambda\) is the critical load of the reversed load. \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) is computed in one of two ways, see Element matrices and internal forces:

  • update_KG() with the solution \(\boldsymbol{u}_0\) of the linear static analysis of the reference load: the membrane forces are those of the plate with its boundary conditions and the actual distribution of the load;

  • update_KG_cte_N() with membrane forces given for each element, positive in tension, without a static analysis: \(\lambda\) multiplies these forces, e.g. Nxx = -1 in every element gives \(\lambda\) equal to the critical compressive force per unit length.

The second is exact for a pre-buckling state of uniform membrane forces; the first represents non-uniform states, e.g. from nodal forces or constrained in-plane displacements, whose load multipliers therefore differ slightly from those of the uniform state.

\(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) is positive definite and \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) symmetric and indefinite, such that Eq. (6.2) is a symmetric generalised eigenvalue problem. With \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) as the positive definite matrix of the pencil it is written \(\boldsymbol{K}_G \boldsymbol{\phi} = \mu \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} \boldsymbol{\phi}\), \(\mu = -1/\lambda\), the form that the tests solve with scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh (A = KG, M = KC0, shift-invert mode 'cayley'), recovering \(\lambda = -1/\mu\). With a follower pressure the problem becomes Eq. (4.8), unsymmetric in general.

The classical buckling coefficients verify this formulation: for a simply supported plate of sides \(a\) and \(b\) under a uniform compression \(N_{xx}\) [TimoshenkoGere1961]

(6.3)#\[N_{xx,cr} = k_c \frac{\pi^2 D}{b^2}, \qquad k_c = \left( \frac{m b}{a} + \frac{a}{m b} \right)^2\]

with \(D\) of Eq. (1.4) and \(m\) the number of half-waves along \(x\), \(k_c = 4\) for a square plate, checked in tests/test_linear_buckling_iso_SSSS.py.

Limits. The linear buckling analysis assumes a pre-buckling state without rotations. For \(\boldsymbol{B} \neq \boldsymbol{0}\), e.g. unsymmetric laminates, or for loads with a transverse part, the pre-buckling state deflects, \(\boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}\) and the second-order terms are not null, and Eq. (6.2) is an approximation of the nonlinear response, to be checked with the nonlinear static analysis. The critical load does not describe the post-buckling behaviour, nor the sensitivity to imperfections.

6.3. Natural frequencies#

The kinetic energy of Eq. (3.8) and the linear strain energy give the equations of free vibration \(\boldsymbol{M}\ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), whose harmonic solutions \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{\phi} \sin \omega_n t\) satisfy

(6.4)#\[(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} - \omega_n^2 \boldsymbol{M}) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

with \(\omega_n\) the natural angular frequencies, in radians per unit time, \(f_n = \omega_n/(2\pi)\), and \(\boldsymbol{\phi}\) the modes. Both matrices are symmetric and positive definite once the rigid-body motions are constrained, e.g. solved with scipy.linalg.eigh or with scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh. update_M() needs h and rho of each element and no integration points. For a simply supported isotropic plate, without the rotary inertia, the mode \(w = \sin(m\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/b)\) gives \(\omega_{mn} = \pi^2 (m^2/a^2 + n^2/b^2) \sqrt{D/(\rho h)}\), checked in tests/test_natural_frequency.py. The geometric stiffness matrix of a static pre-stress, added to \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\), gives the frequencies of the pre-stressed plate, which vanish at the buckling load.

6.4. Nonlinear static analysis#

For deflections of the order of the thickness or larger, the membrane stretching of the von Kármán strains carries part of the load and the equilibrium

(6.5)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}, \lambda) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

is solved for the displacements at increasing values of the load factor \(\lambda\). In the Newton-Raphson method each iteration solves

(6.6)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_T \Delta\boldsymbol{u} = -\boldsymbol{R}, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_T = \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{u}) + \boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{u})\]

with the tangent stiffness matrix of update_KC0(), update_KCNL() and update_KG(), all computed with the current displacements and the same integration points as \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\) of update_fint(). Since \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) is the exact Jacobian of \(\boldsymbol{F}_{int}\), see Element matrices and internal forces, the iterations converge quadratically near the solution, as asserted in tests/test_static_axial_load_nonlinear.py. The value arrays of KCNL and KG are zeroed before each assembly, since the functions add to them. A follower pressure adds its force vector to the residual and its load stiffness matrix to \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\), Eq. (4.7).

Under load control the iterations fail at a limit point, where \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) becomes singular; beyond it, path-following (arc-length) methods are needed. A perfect plate under in-plane compression stays flat on the trivial path: the nonlinear analysis of buckling and post-buckling needs a small perturbation, e.g. a small transverse load or an initial deflection, that selects the buckled path. Below the buckling load, the response to a small transverse load \(\boldsymbol{F}_t\) superposed on the in-plane load \(P\) tends to the linearised solution \((\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_G(P)) \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{F}_t\), with \(\boldsymbol{K}_G\) of the in-plane load alone, amplified as the load approaches the buckling load.

6.5. Solving with structsolve#

The solvers of structsolve receive the matrices and vectors of the model assembled by the user. It is used by the tests and not required by bfscplate2d, see Installing bfscplate2d. The linear buckling and natural frequency problems are solved by structsolve.lb(Kuu, KGuu), which returns the load multipliers \(\lambda\), and structsolve.freq(Kuu, Muu), which returns \(-\omega_n^2\).

The nonlinear static analysis is solved by structsolve.Analysis, with four callables that loop over the elements, call the functions of the element and assemble:

  • calc_fext(inc=1., silent=False), the external forces \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\) times the load factor inc;

  • calc_fint(c, silent=False), the internal forces of update_fint() at the displacements c;

  • calc_kC(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=False), \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) with NLgeom=False, and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL}(\boldsymbol{c}) + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL}(\boldsymbol{c})\) with NLgeom=True;

  • calc_kG(c=None, NLgeom=False, silent=False), \(\boldsymbol{K}_G(\boldsymbol{c})\) of update_KG(), called with NLgeom=True;

such that calc_kC + calc_kG is \(\boldsymbol{K}_T\) and the residual of structsolve is Eq. (6.5), with the corrections of Eq. (6.6), the convention of structsolve. The load factor goes from 0 to 1 in increments, the first of size Analysis.initialInc and the next ones growing up to Analysis.maxInc, each starting from a predictor extrapolated from the previous increment, the linear solution of \(\boldsymbol{K}_{C0}\) in the first one; Analysis.static returns the load factors and the solutions of the converged increments.

structsolve imposes no boundary condition itself. The constrained degrees of freedom are removed either by the partition of Assembly, loads and boundary conditions, the callables receiving c = u[bu] and returning vectors and matrices restricted to bu, or by returning the vectors and matrices of the whole model with the rows and columns of the constrained degrees of freedom zeroed in all the matrices, which the solvers remove.

The convergence criterion is, by default, Crisfield’s: the residual and the reference forces, the external and the internal forces, are scaled with the diagonal of the linear stiffness matrix calc_kC() over the free degrees of freedom, and the iterations stop when their ratio is below Analysis.relTOL = 1e-9. The scaled norm does not depend on the units of the degrees of freedom, which here mix displacements, slopes and \(w_{,xy}\), see The BFSC plate element; Analysis.convergence = 'relative' selects the Euclidean norm instead.

A follower pressure \(\lambda p\) enters through the optional keyword argument inc, the load factor, which structsolve passes to the callables that declare it: calc_fext gives the dead part \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0})\), calc_fint subtracts the part of update_fext_follower() with reference=0 for the pressure inc*p, and calc_kC adds the load stiffness matrix of update_KCfollower() for inc*p, such that the residual and the tangent are those of Eq. (4.7). The tangent stiffness matrix is then unsymmetric in general, which the solvers of structsolve support with LU factorisations. For the arc-length methods, Analysis.NL_method, calc_fext also declares the configuration c and returns the follower force vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{c})\) of p, minus the derivative of the residual with respect to the load factor.

6.6. Choosing an analysis and its parameters#

  • Linear static for deflections small compared with the thickness; for larger ones the membrane stiffening, absent from the linear solution, reduces the deflections under transverse loads, and the nonlinear analysis is needed.

  • Linear buckling for the critical loads of flat plates under in-plane loads; update_KG_cte_N() when the pre-buckling membrane forces are known and uniform per element, update_KG() with a static solution otherwise.

  • Nonlinear static for large deflections, post-buckling with a perturbation, follower pressures, and to check a linear buckling analysis of a plate whose pre-buckling state deflects.

  • Integration points: nint = 4 integrates KC0 and the constant-force KG exactly, nint = 5 the KG of a static solution, nint = 7 the internal forces and KCNL, see Element matrices and internal forces; the same points for every function of one analysis.

  • Mesh: the accuracy of buckling loads and frequencies depends on the number of elements per half-wave of the modes of interest, to be checked by refining the mesh. Boundary conditions constraining only the nodal values, without the derivatives along the edge, converge with the mesh, see Assembly, loads and boundary conditions.
