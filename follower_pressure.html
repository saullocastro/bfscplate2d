4. Follower pressure#

A pressure of a gas or a liquid stays normal to the deformed plate and acts on its deformed area, as opposed to a dead pressure, which keeps the direction \(z\) and the undeformed area. For large deflections the difference matters: the force vector of a follower pressure depends on the displacements, and its derivative, the load stiffness matrix, enters the tangent stiffness matrix [Hibbitt1979] [SchweizerhofRamm1984]. This chapter derives the two quantities computed by the library:

  • update_fext_follower(), the force vector \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u})\) of one element, added to a global force vector;

  • update_KCfollower(), the load stiffness matrix \(\boldsymbol{K}_F = -\partial \boldsymbol{F}_p/\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e\) of one element, written in sparse arrays, see Assembly, loads and boundary conditions.

4.1. Virtual work and area vector#

The pressure \(p(x, y)\) is given per unit area of the undeformed plate, as a function of the undeformed coordinates. With \(\boldsymbol{X} = \boldsymbol{X}_0 + \boldsymbol{u}\) the deformed position of the reference surface, \(\boldsymbol{u} = \{u, v, w\}^\top\), its virtual work is

(4.1)#\[\begin{split}\delta W_p = \int_A p \, \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \delta\boldsymbol{u} \, dx \, dy, \qquad \boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{X}_{,x} \times \boldsymbol{X}_{,y}, \qquad \boldsymbol{X}_{,x} = \begin{Bmatrix} 1 + u_{,x} \\ v_{,x} \\ w_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{X}_{,y} = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,y} \\ 1 + v_{,y} \\ w_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

The area vector \(\boldsymbol{a}\) is normal to the deformed surface, and its length is the ratio between the deformed and the undeformed areas. The cross product is bilinear in the displacement gradients; split by orders, \(\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1 + \boldsymbol{a}_2\),

(4.2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{a}_0 = \begin{Bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{a}_1 = \begin{Bmatrix} -w_{,x} \\ -w_{,y} \\ u_{,x} + v_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{a}_2 = \begin{Bmatrix} v_{,x} w_{,y} - w_{,x} v_{,y} \\ w_{,x} u_{,y} - u_{,x} w_{,y} \\ u_{,x} v_{,y} - v_{,x} u_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

\(\boldsymbol{a}_0\) gives the dead pressure; \(\boldsymbol{a}_1\) contains the rotation of the normal, \((-w_{,x}, -w_{,y})\), and the change of area \(u_{,x} + v_{,y}\).

4.2. Consistent truncation#

With the moderate rotations of the von Kármán strains of Plate theory, \(u_{,x} \sim \epsilon\) and \(w_{,x} \sim \epsilon^{1/2}\), with \(\epsilon\) the order of the strains, such that \(\delta u \sim \epsilon^{1/2} \delta w\). \(\boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1\) then keeps every term of \(p \, \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \delta\boldsymbol{u}\) up to the order \(\epsilon \, p \, \delta w\), while \(\boldsymbol{a}_2\) adds terms of the order \(\epsilon^2 p \, \delta w\), of the order of the terms that the strains neglect. The first-order area vector, quadratic=0 (the default), is therefore the truncation consistent with the kinematics: the force vector is affine in the displacements and the load stiffness matrix constant, computed once for a given pressure. quadratic=1 uses the complete area vector, e.g. to check the sensitivity of a result to the truncation; the load stiffness matrix then depends on the displacements and is recomputed at each iteration.

4.3. Force vector#

Discretised with the rows of Eq. (2.5) and the quadrature of Element matrices and internal forces, Eq. (4.1) gives the element force vector of update_fext_follower()

(4.3)#\[\boldsymbol{F}_p = \int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top a_x + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top a_y + (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top a_z \right) dA, \qquad \boldsymbol{a} = r \, \boldsymbol{a}_0 + \boldsymbol{a}_1 + q \, \boldsymbol{a}_2\]

with \(q = 1\) for quadratic not 0 and \(r = 1\) for reference not 0 (the default), otherwise 0. At \(\boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\), \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0}) = \int_A p (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top dA\) is the consistent load vector of the dead pressure. reference=0 leaves this term out and gives \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}) - \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0})\), the part that vanishes in the undeformed state.

The pressure is given to the functions at the integration points of each element, a C-contiguous array p of shape (nint, nint), p[i, j] at xi = points[i] and eta = points[j], already multiplied by the load factor if there is one. A positive p pushes along \(+z\) in the undeformed state, like a positive force on \(w\).

4.4. Load stiffness matrix#

The derivative of Eq. (4.3) gives the load stiffness matrix of update_KCfollower()

(4.4)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_F = -\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}_p}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} = -\int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top \frac{\partial a_x}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \frac{\partial a_y}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} + (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \frac{\partial a_z}{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e} \right) dA\]

For the first-order area vector, \(\partial \boldsymbol{a}_1/\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e\) has the constant rows \(-\boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x}\), \(-\boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y}\) and \(\boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} + \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y}\), and

(4.5)#\[\boldsymbol{K}_F = \int_A p \left( (\boldsymbol{S}^u)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,x} + (\boldsymbol{S}^v)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^w_{,y} - (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^u_{,x} - (\boldsymbol{S}^w)^\top \boldsymbol{S}^v_{,y} \right) dA\]

independent of the displacements, with entries in the blocks \([u, w]\), \([v, w]\), \([w, u]\) and \([w, v]\). The complete area vector adds \(\partial \boldsymbol{a}_2/\partial \boldsymbol{u}_e\), linear in the displacement gradients, to these blocks and to the blocks \([u, v]\) and \([v, u]\). The function writes all six blocks, KCfollower_SPARSE_SIZE entries per element, the values of \([u, v]\) and \([v, u]\) being zero for quadratic=0.

4.5. Symmetry and conservativeness#

A load derived from a potential has a symmetric load stiffness matrix. For the first-order area vector, \(\boldsymbol{\delta}^\top \boldsymbol{K}_F \boldsymbol{\Delta} = -P(\delta, \Delta)\) for two displacement fields \(\delta\) and \(\Delta\), with the bilinear form \(P(\delta, \Delta) = \int_A p \, \delta\boldsymbol{u} \cdot \boldsymbol{a}_1(\Delta\boldsymbol{u}) \, dA\). Its skew part collects into derivatives, \(p \, \partial_x (\Delta u \, \delta w - \delta u \, \Delta w)\) and the same along \(y\), and an integration by parts gives

(4.6)#\[P(\delta, \Delta) - P(\Delta, \delta) = - \oint p \left[ (\delta u \, \Delta w - \delta w \, \Delta u) n_x + (\delta v \, \Delta w - \delta w \, \Delta v) n_y \right] ds + \int_A \left[ p_{,x} (\delta u \, \Delta w - \delta w \, \Delta u) + p_{,y} (\delta v \, \Delta w - \delta w \, \Delta v) \right] dA\]

with \(\{n_x, n_y\}\) the outward normal of the boundary of the loaded region. The load stiffness matrix is therefore symmetric, and the follower pressure conservative, if and only if

  • \(p\) is uniform over the loaded region, and

  • at each point of the boundary of the loaded region either \(w = 0\) or the in-plane displacement normal to the boundary vanishes, \(u = 0\) on the edges \(x\) = constant and \(v = 0\) on the edges \(y\) = constant.

Simply supported and clamped edges and symmetry planes meet the second condition; the load potential is then the pressure times the change of the enclosed volume. Free or partially restrained edges, a pressure on a patch whose edges lie inside the plate and a pressure that varies over the plate give an unsymmetric matrix, the result of [Hibbitt1979] and [SchweizerhofRamm1984] for closed surfaces and for loaded surfaces with a fixed boundary.

The boundary of one element is the boundary of its loaded region, where the second condition does not hold: the element matrix is unsymmetric. In the assembled matrix the contour integrals of the edges shared by two elements cancel when \(p\) is continuous, since \(u\), \(v\) and \(w\) are continuous, see The BFSC plate element, and the conditions apply to the boundary of the loaded region of the plate. The matrix is written in full, not as a triangle, and must not be symmetrised: the linear systems and eigenvalue problems that contain it need solvers of unsymmetric matrices. These conditions are those of the first-order area vector; for quadratic=1 the matrix depends on the displacements.

4.6. Residual and tangent stiffness matrix#

With the pressure \(\lambda p\) of a load factor \(\lambda\), the residual of the nonlinear static analysis of Analyses and its Jacobian are

(4.7)#\[\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u}) - \lambda \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}), \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_T = \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \boldsymbol{K}_{CNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_{GNL} + \boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_F, \qquad \boldsymbol{K}_F = \lambda \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\]

with \(\boldsymbol{F}_p\) and \(\boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}\) of the same p and quadratic. Since the functions receive the pressure already multiplied by \(\lambda\), the force vector and the load stiffness matrix of \(\lambda p\) are computed by giving them lbd*p. Writing \(\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0}) + (\boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{u}) - \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0}))\), a solver whose external force vector is fixed, \(\lambda \boldsymbol{F}_{ext}\), takes the dead part \(\boldsymbol{F}_{ext} = \boldsymbol{F}_p(\boldsymbol{0})\) as external forces and the part of reference=0, times \(\lambda\), subtracted from the internal forces, see Analyses.

A linear buckling analysis under a follower pressure \(\lambda p\) adds the load stiffness matrix of p to the geometric stiffness matrix of the linear static solution of p:

(4.8)#\[\left( \boldsymbol{K}_{C0} + \lambda (\boldsymbol{K}_G + \boldsymbol{K}_{F_{ref}}) \right) \boldsymbol{\phi} = \boldsymbol{0}\]

an unsymmetric eigenvalue problem unless the conditions above hold.

4.7. Numerical integration#

For a pressure constant over the element the integrands of Eqs. (4.3) and (4.4) are polynomials of degree at most 6 in \(\xi\) and in \(\eta\) for quadratic=0 and 8 for quadratic=1, integrated exactly with nint >= 4 and nint >= 5.

The force vector, the load stiffness matrix, its symmetry conditions and the consistency of the tangent stiffness matrix are verified in tests/test_follower_pressure.py.
