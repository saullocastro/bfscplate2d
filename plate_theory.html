1. Plate theory#

This chapter states the plate theory that every function of bfscplate2d.bfscplate2d discretises: the classical laminated plate theory with the nonlinear strains of von Kármán [Reddy2003]. The element that approximates it is the subject of The BFSC plate element, and the matrices that follow from it of Element matrices and internal forces.

1.1. Coordinates and sign conventions#

The plate lies in the plane \(x\)-\(y\) of a right-handed Cartesian system, with \(z\) normal to the plate. The reference surface is \(z = 0\); the displacements of its points along \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) are \(u(x, y)\), \(v(x, y)\) and \(w(x, y)\). Subscripts after a comma denote partial derivatives, \(w_{,x} = \partial w/\partial x\), as in the names of the code (w_x, Sw_x, …). With this convention:

  • the membrane forces \(\boldsymbol{N} = \{N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}\}^\top\) (Nxx, Nyy, Nxy), forces per unit length, are positive in tension;

  • the moments \(\boldsymbol{M} = \{M_{xx}, M_{yy}, M_{xy}\}^\top\) (Mxx, Myy, Mxy), moments per unit length, are the first moments \(\int \sigma z \, dz\) of the stresses through the thickness;

  • a positive transverse force or pressure acts along \(+z\), like a positive \(w\).

The library has no units: the lengths, the forces and the masses of a model are given in a consistent system, e.g. N, m and kg, and every result is in that system.

1.2. Kinematics#

The classical laminated plate theory keeps the Kirchhoff hypotheses: the normals to the reference surface remain straight, unstretched and normal to the deformed reference surface, i.e. without transverse shear strains \(\gamma_{xz}\) and \(\gamma_{yz}\) and with \(\epsilon_{zz} = 0\). The normal rotates by \(-w_{,x}\) about \(y\) and by \(-w_{,y}\) about \(x\), and the displacements of a point at the distance \(z\) from the reference surface are [Reddy2003]

(1.1)#\[u(x, y, z) = u - z w_{,x}, \qquad v(x, y, z) = v - z w_{,y}, \qquad w(x, y, z) = w\]

The strain-displacement relations of a plate contain the squares and products of all the displacement gradients. For small strains and moderate rotations, the assumptions of von Kármán, only the squares and products of the slopes \(w_{,x}\) and \(w_{,y}\) are kept: the slopes may be of the order of the square root of the strains, whereas the in-plane gradients \(u_{,x}\), \(u_{,y}\), \(v_{,x}\), \(v_{,y}\) are of the order of the strains, and their squares are neglected. The strains at \(z\) are then \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} + z \boldsymbol{\kappa}\), with the membrane strains and the curvatures of the reference surface

(1.2)#\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \begin{Bmatrix} \epsilon_{xx} \\ \epsilon_{yy} \\ \gamma_{xy} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} u_{,x} \\ v_{,y} \\ u_{,y} + v_{,x} \end{Bmatrix} + \begin{Bmatrix} w_{,x}^2/2 \\ w_{,y}^2/2 \\ w_{,x} w_{,y} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\kappa} = \begin{Bmatrix} \kappa_{xx} \\ \kappa_{yy} \\ \kappa_{xy} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{Bmatrix} -w_{,xx} \\ -w_{,yy} \\ -2 w_{,xy} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

\(\gamma_{xy}\) is the engineering shear strain. The first vector of \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\) is the linear membrane strain, the second the nonlinear one, which couples the membrane and the bending behaviour: a deflection stretches the reference surface, and membrane forces change the bending stiffness. The curvatures are linear in \(w\). Without the nonlinear part, Eq. (1.2) is the linear theory, in which the membrane and the bending problems are coupled only through the matrix \(\boldsymbol{B}\) below.

1.3. Constitutive relation#

For a laminate of linear elastic plies, the integration of the plane-stress relation of each ply through the thickness gives the membrane forces and the moments [Reddy2003]

(1.3)#\[\begin{split}\begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{N} \\ \boldsymbol{M} \end{Bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{A} & \boldsymbol{B} \\ \boldsymbol{B} & \boldsymbol{D} \end{bmatrix} \begin{Bmatrix} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \\ \boldsymbol{\kappa} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

with the extensional stiffness \(\boldsymbol{A}\) (force per unit length), the extension-bending coupling \(\boldsymbol{B}\) (force) and the bending stiffness \(\boldsymbol{D}\) (force times length), three symmetric \(3 \times 3\) matrices. The \(6 \times 6\) matrix is the ABD attribute of BFSCPlate2D, in the order of Eq. (1.3), e.g. the ABD attribute of a laminate of composites. The functions read only the entries A11, A12, A16, A22, A26, A66 on and above the diagonal of \(\boldsymbol{A}\), and likewise of \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\); the matrix is taken as symmetric.

\(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\) for a laminate symmetric about the reference surface, which then separates the linear membrane and bending problems. A reference surface that is not the mid-surface, e.g. an offset plate, enters only through \(\boldsymbol{A}\), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) and \(\boldsymbol{D}\) computed about that surface; the mass matrix, however, assumes the mid-surface, see Element matrices and internal forces. For an isotropic plate of Young’s modulus \(E\), Poisson’s ratio \(\nu\) and thickness \(h\), \(\boldsymbol{B} = \boldsymbol{0}\), \(A_{11} = E h/(1 - \nu^2)\) and the bending stiffness is

(1.4)#\[D_{11} = D = \frac{E h^3}{12 (1 - \nu^2)}\]

1.4. Strain energy and equilibrium#

The strain energy of the plate is

(1.5)#\[U = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{\epsilon} + 2 \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^\top \boldsymbol{B} \boldsymbol{\kappa} + \boldsymbol{\kappa}^\top \boldsymbol{D} \boldsymbol{\kappa} \right) dA = \frac{1}{2} \int_A \left( \boldsymbol{N}^\top \boldsymbol{\epsilon} + \boldsymbol{M}^\top \boldsymbol{\kappa} \right) dA\]

and its first variation, with \(\delta\boldsymbol{\kappa}\) linear in \(\delta w\) and

(1.6)#\[\begin{split}\delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \begin{Bmatrix} \delta u_{,x} + w_{,x} \delta w_{,x} \\ \delta v_{,y} + w_{,y} \delta w_{,y} \\ \delta u_{,y} + \delta v_{,x} + w_{,x} \delta w_{,y} + w_{,y} \delta w_{,x} \end{Bmatrix}\end{split}\]

is the internal virtual work \(\delta U = \int_A (\boldsymbol{N}^\top \delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon} + \boldsymbol{M}^\top \delta\boldsymbol{\kappa}) \, dA\). The equilibrium of the plate is the principle of virtual work, \(\delta U = \delta W\) for every admissible variation, with \(\delta W\) the virtual work of the external forces. For loads that do not depend on the displacements, \(\delta W\) is linear in the variations; a pressure that follows the deformation makes it depend on the displacements, see Follower pressure.

Two features of \(\delta U\) determine the element and its matrices:

  • \(\boldsymbol{\kappa}\) contains the second derivatives of \(w\), such that the energy is finite for an approximation of \(w\) only when \(w\) and its first derivatives are continuous over the plate, see The BFSC plate element;

  • the products \(w_{,x} \delta w_{,x}\), … of Eq. (1.6) make the internal forces nonlinear in the displacements, and the derivative of the term \(\boldsymbol{N}^\top \delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\) with respect to the displacements at constant \(\boldsymbol{N}\) is the geometric stiffness, the term that governs buckling, see Element matrices and internal forces.

1.5. Limits of validity#

  • Thin plates. Without transverse shear deformation the theory overestimates the stiffness, and the buckling loads and natural frequencies, of plates whose thickness is not small compared with the in-plane dimensions and with the wavelengths of the deformation, and of laminates or sandwich plates of low transverse shear stiffness.

  • Moderate rotations. The strains of Eq. (1.2) hold for small strains, slopes \(w_{,x}\), \(w_{,y}\) small compared with one, and small in-plane rotations. They describe the membrane stiffening of plates deflecting by several thicknesses, but not large rotations.

  • Linear elasticity. The ABD matrix is constant: no plasticity, damage or temperature dependence.

  • Flat plates. The reference surface is a plane; curvature of the undeformed surface, as in shells, is not represented.
